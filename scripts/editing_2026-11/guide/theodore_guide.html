Option A

LIVE TITLE OVERLAY

NOT BUILT
What it does

Replaces the 47 baked overlay images with one editable Fusion title in Resolve. The title shows the angle ID, whether it is a new angle or a return, the angle type, lens, lighting, movement, a replicate-it tip and the angle grid, all as live text that slides in on each new angle over a frosted-glass blur.

What it can do
  • Show angle ID, NEW or BACK-TO-THIS-ANGLE, type, lens, light and movement as text fields
  • Fill the angle grid (sizes by heights) with a count of how often each angle was used
  • Animate in only when the angle changes, never on every cut or camera move
  • Wear any of the three skins: Neon, Hollywood or Full Frame
  • Be restyled once in the Inspector so every angle updates together
Why it is useful
  • Fixing a typo takes seconds instead of re-making 47 images
  • The look stops feeling stale and can match each class or brand
  • Students read the same data in a cleaner, moving layout
In practice

Change the wording of one replicate-it tip, or the font of the whole overlay, and all 47 angle changes update at once.

Theodore needs

Nothing new. Pick a skin: Neon, Hollywood or Full Frame.

Effort and status


Today the overlay is baked images; the live version is a design only.

Watch out

The Fusion template plus a script to fill it per angle is the tricky part. Theodore tests on three angles before doing all of them.

Option B

COVERAGE TRACKER

PARTLY BUILT
What it does

Gives every angle slot keywords (like WIDE, LOW) and a status colour, then keeps a live list of the angles still to shoot. As takes are assigned, the angle grid fills in, so anyone can see what coverage exists and what is missing.

What it can do
  • Tag each slot with size, height and movement keywords that Resolve can search
  • Colour a slot for planned, shot, selected, synced or graded
  • List the angles not yet covered, with the gaps shown in the angle grid
  • Keep all of it inside the project, so it travels with the timeline
  • Show a scene's angle diversity at a glance
Why it is useful
  • On shoot day the crew sees what is still missing before they wrap
  • Students learn to plan variety, not just repeat one angle
  • A producer can read progress without opening a single clip
In practice

Mid-shoot you ask for the list and see OTS and low-angle wide are still empty, so you shoot them before moving on.

Theodore needs

The shot list, and which angle types matter to you.

Effort and status


Metadata and status colours already work in Resolve.

Watch out

The scripting API Theodore checked cannot create Smart Bins, so the saved-search bin is a one-time click for you.

Say it"Build B for the John Wick angles."

Option C

COMPARE MODE

NOT BUILT
What it does

Puts a student's take beside the reference for each angle. Resolve's Color page can wipe or split-screen a still against a clip, and the waveform and parade scopes show whether the lighting matches. Theodore sets up the stills and the layout, so the check takes seconds.

What it can do
  • Load the reference still for each slot, ready next to the take
  • Set up a wipe or split so framing and height can be compared
  • Use the scopes to compare how bright and how colourful the take is
  • Give a four-line checklist per angle: framing, height, lens, light
  • Keep the reference stills private and out of any student document
Why it is useful
  • Turns "does it look right?" into a quick, visible check
  • Gives students specific feedback on what to change for the next take
  • Builds the habit of matching an angle on purpose
In practice

A student's take is too high and too bright; the wipe shows the gap and the scopes show how far off the lighting is.

Theodore needs

Reference stills (kept private) and the student takes.

Effort and status


Not built. The layout and scopes are standard Resolve features.

Watch out

Wiping is a Resolve screen feature. Theodore sets up the stills and layout but cannot drive the wipe from a script. Film frames never go into a student guide.

Say it"Build C, here are my reference stills."

Option D

CUT & CAMERA CHECK

NOT BUILT
What it does

A second opinion on the cut list and on camera movement. Theodore cross-checks its own cuts against Resolve's built-in Scene Cut Detection, and uses Fusion's camera tracker to measure pans, tilts and pushes instead of guessing the movement row.

What it can do
  • Compare detected cuts against Theodore's list and flag every disagreement
  • Catch false cuts: door swings, flashes, whip pans, light flicker
  • Measure camera movement from the picture, not from a guess
  • Shrink the review to a short list of spots for you to judge
  • Re-run after you fix an angle, to confirm the list still holds
Why it is useful
  • Fewer wrong angle changes, so the overlay and guide can be trusted
  • Objective movement labels students can reproduce
  • Saves you scrubbing the whole scene to find mistakes
In practice

Resolve sees a cut where a door swings; Theodore flags it, you confirm it is one shot, and the angle count is corrected.

Theodore needs

Only the footage already in Resolve.

Effort and status


Not built, and untested on very dark, flickering footage.

Watch out

Every detector gets fooled in the dark, so you stay the final judge. Three false cuts were already fixed by hand.

Say it"Run D on the scene."

Option E

SOUND ROW

NOT BUILT
What it does

Uses Resolve's own transcription and audio classification to fill the SOUND field for every shot: dialogue, gunfire, music, room tone. The sound row becomes real data with timecodes instead of "unverified".

What it can do
  • Transcribe speech with timecodes
  • Label dialogue, effects, music and room tone per shot
  • Fill the SOUND field in the overlay, the sheet and the guide
  • Flag shots whose sound it cannot identify instead of guessing
  • Give students sound cues they can recreate with simple gear
Why it is useful
  • Sound is half the scene, and now it is studied like the picture
  • Searchable transcript to find a line or a cue fast
  • Honest labels: measured where possible, UNVERIFIED where not
In practice

Shot 12 is tagged dialogue under low music; the guide tells students to record the line close and add a quiet bed.

Theodore needs

A legitimate copy that has audio. The screen recording is silent.

Effort and status


Not built. The calls exist in this Resolve's scripting API but have not been tried.

Watch out

Audio from a film copy stays private study material, never in a student guide.

SHEET + GUIDE EXPORT

DATA READY
What it does

Turns the angle log into two documents: a shot list sheet with one row per shot (ID, in, out, size, angle, movement, lens, light, action, sound) and a student guide with timecodes, written descriptions and low-budget how-to tips. Written descriptions only, never film frames.

What it can do
  • Export a sortable spreadsheet of every shot and angle
  • Build a designed PDF guide like this one, organised by angle
  • Add generic, low-budget tips for each angle type
  • Mark anything unverified clearly so students are not misled
  • Regenerate both documents any time the angle log changes
Why it is useful
  • Handouts and a shot list production can use on set
  • Students study the scene from words and timecodes, not copied frames
  • One source of truth: fix the log once, both documents update
In practice

Hand students the PDF to study angle by angle, and give the crew the sheet sorted by size to plan the shoot.

Theodore needs

Your pass through the angle markers first, so it is built on a clean list.

Effort and status


The data is ready; the two documents are not built yet.

Watch out

The tips are generic how-tos, not claims about how the film was made.

Why it is useful
  • A project is ready in minutes instead of hours of setup
  • Consistent slating means takes can be matched later
  • Students see the full plan as a coloured, labelled timeline

Option H

SWAP-IN LOOP

DRY-RUN ONLY
What it does

The on-set DIT role. Theodore reads the slate on each take with Resolve's Slate ID, matches it to its placeholder, drops it in at the planned length with the lavs synced and linked, and keeps unused takes in the bin. The timeline builds itself as you shoot.

What it can do
  • Match each take to its shot by slate, shot ID and roll order
  • Sync lav audio to camera, checking the start and end for drift
  • Keep each transmitter's own offset, and link audio under the video
  • Show planned length against actual length on every shot
  • Flag unclear slates for you instead of guessing
Why it is useful
  • A first assembly exists by the time the shoot wraps
  • You see at once which shots are covered and which are not
  • Placeholders from Option G or J turn into real footage in place
In practice

You slate S04, shoot three takes, and S04 fills with take 3 at the planned length while takes 1 and 2 wait in the bin.

Theodore needs

A few real slated takes and the lav files to test on safely.

Effort and status


Only the read-only matcher exists (dry run).

Watch out

Whether Resolve's slate reader fills Scene and Take from a script is untested. Viewing all takes as one clip is under test (see Option J).

Say it"Set up H, here are my slated takes."

Option I · Recap method

THE MR.H METHOD

Your content, cut the way Mr.H would cut it. The story is the job: a hook, an arc, and only the lines that move your audience toward the goal. You give a short brief (brand, brand goal, piece goal, audience) and Theodore offers up to three different story options. Length and style stay flexible; B-roll and polish come after.

1

TRANSCRIBE & TAG

Transcribe all the lav audio before cutting. Tag every sentence as intro / résumé / logistics, or as a value statement.

2

PICK THE VALUE LINES

Keep only the value lines that serve the brief. The story sets the length (the CFF Recap kept about 15%). Names, titles and "what we do" stay on the floor.

3

BUILD THE SPINE

Purpose, gratitude, belief, a proof story, giving back, call to action, then a closing line. Search every recording for the closing line.

4

PLAN THE PACING

Fast bites early, longer statements later. Music swells with quick B-roll between the blocks.

5

SPLICE & COVER

Splice at the sentence level with butt joins (no pause). Cover jumps with 1.5-second B-roll.

6

SCORE & CLOSE

Duck the music to about −16 dB under speech, swell it between blocks, and end with a long fade.

Gate 1 · before building

You see 1 to 3 story options side by side (hook, arc, length, who it speaks to, every line in order). Pick one, mix, or build all.

Gate 2 · after building

Theodore transcribes its own result and checks it against the spine.

How it builds
  • You give the brief: goal, length, audience, the music track

Who it helps

  • Directors: see the finished piece, order and reference frames before a camera rolls

Option K

DIT

AI DIT · PROVEN ON ONE PROJECT
What it does

Everything you would hire a DIT for, done by Theodore. Upload what you just shot, plus your music, score, transitions and assets: it syncs each lav to camera, finds the takes, files everything beside the raw footage, and builds one multicam per shot (Scene 1A, 1B ...), every take an angle with its own lav.

What it can do
  • Sync lav audio to camera, measured and placed frame-accurately
  • Sort music, score, transitions, SFX and graphics into Project Assets, import them to Resolve
  • Place any asset where you say, such as the score under Scene 1C
  • Find the takes in a shot, build a multicam per shot, name it Scene 1A Take 2
  • Reopen Resolve and your project after a crash
Why it is useful
  • No DIT on the payroll, and no waiting on a wrangler
  • The next scene and your assets are already on the timeline when you start shooting again
  • Choosing a take is a click, and its audio comes with it
In practice

You shoot Scene 1C, upload the card and the score cue, and say "score under 1C". By 1D, 1C is in place with its takes as angles and the score under it.

Theodore needs

Where footage lands, how you slate, your assets, and Resolve open with the bridge running.

Effort and status


Proven on one project; asset sorting and placing tested. Not built: folder watcher, checksums, backups.

Watch out

Resolve's built-in transitions cannot be added by script (uploaded transition clips can). Not yet a safe card backup.

Say it"DIT: here is the footage and the score for scene 1C."

House rules

What Theodore never does

  • No surprise renders. Nothing is rendered unless you ask.
  • No deleting. Old builds are renamed as checkpoints and filed in Timelines/Working.
  • Read-back after every build. Clip counts, gaps and markers are checked, then shown.
  • Stops when Resolve stops. If Resolve refuses writes, Theodore tells you instead of forcing it.
  • Lavs travel with the video. Every cut carries its mic audio, linked.
  • Honest labels. If a shot cannot be seen clearly, it says UNVERIFIED rather than guessing.
  • Film frames stay private. They never go into a student guide or sheet.

Straight talk

Known limits

  • Cuts in the dark. Door swings, whip pans and light flicker fool the detector. Three were fixed after you caught them; more may remain.
  • Angle height. Defaults to eye level when the camera height cannot be seen.
  • Sound. The screen recording has no audio, so sound is unverified.
  • The John Wick study uses a fan upload recorded on screen. It is private study material, not for students.
  • GitHub is connected for code and guides. Resolve, footage and transcription still need this Mac on.
  • Chrome setting. "Allow JavaScript from Apple Events" is on for the video work. You can switch it off in View > Developer.
