How it works

Pick it. Say the letter. Watch it appear.

You talk to Theodore here, in chat. Theodore drives Resolve for you, builds the thing, then reads it back to prove it landed. No panel to learn.

01

02

03
VERIFY

Theodore builds it in Resolve, checks clip counts, gaps and markers, shows you a screenshot, and tells you plainly what is still a guess.

A
LIVE TITLE OVERLAY
NOT BUILT
What it does

Replaces the baked overlay images with one Fusion title in Resolve: angle ID, type, tip and the angle grid as live text, sliding in on each new angle, with a real frosted-glass blur behind it.

You get
  • Change any word in the Inspector
  • Restyle once, every angle updates
  • A cleaner, animated, less "stale" look
Theodore needs

Nothing new. Pick a skin: Neon, Hollywood or Full Frame.

Effort
Watch out

The Fusion template plus a script to fill it per angle is the tricky part. Theodore tests on three angles before doing all of them.

Say it"Build A in the Hollywood skin."
B
COVERAGE TRACKER
PARTLY BUILT
What it does

Every angle slot gets keywords (like WIDE · LOW) and a status colour. A list shows which angles are still to shoot and which are done, and the angle grid fills as takes are assigned.

You get
  • A live to-do list that lives in the project
  • Planned, shot, selected, synced, graded: one colour each
  • Students see exactly what coverage is missing
Theodore needs

The shot list, and which angle types matter to you.

Effort
Watch out

Metadata and status colours already work. The scripting API Theodore checked cannot create Smart Bins, so the saved-search bin is a one-time click for you.

Say it"Build B for the John Wick angles."
Theodore · Field Guide · Options A and B05
C
COMPARE MODE
NOT BUILT
What it does

Puts a student's take next to the reference for each angle. Resolve's Color page can wipe or split-screen a still against a clip, and the waveform and parade scopes show whether the lighting matches.

You get
  • An instant "did we match the angle?" check
  • A four-line checklist per angle: framing, height, lens, light
  • A reference still loaded and ready for each slot
Theodore needs

Reference stills (kept private) and the student takes.

Effort
Watch out

Wiping is a Resolve screen feature; Theodore sets up the stills and layout but cannot drive the wipe from a script. Film frames never go into a student guide.

Say it"Build C, here are my reference stills."
D
CUT & CAMERA CHECK
NOT BUILT
What it does

Cross-checks Theodore's cut list against Resolve's built-in Scene Cut Detection, and uses Fusion's camera tracker to measure pans, tilts and pushes instead of guessing the movement row.

You get
  • Fewer false cuts (door swings, flashes, whip pans)
  • Objective camera-movement labels
  • A shorter list of spots for you to judge
Theodore needs

Only the footage already in Resolve.

Effort
Watch out

Untested on this very dark, flickering footage. Every detector gets fooled in the dark, so you stay the final judge.

Say it"Run D on the scene."
Theodore · Field Guide · Options C and D06
E
SOUND ROW
NOT BUILT
What it does

Uses Resolve's own transcription and audio classification to fill the SOUND field for every shot: dialogue, gunfire, music, room tone.

You get
  • The sound row as real data, not "unverified"
  • A transcript with timecodes
  • Sound cues students can recreate
Theodore needs

A legitimate copy that has audio. The screen recording is silent.

Effort
Watch out

The calls exist in this Resolve's scripting API but have not been tried yet. Audio from a film copy stays private study material.

Say it"Run E, here is the audio copy."
F
SHEET + GUIDE EXPORT
DATA READY
What it does

Turns the angle log into two documents: a shot list sheet (one row per shot: ID, in, out, size, angle, movement, lens, light, action, sound) and a student guide with timecodes, written descriptions and low-budget tips.

You get
  • A spreadsheet and a PDF like this one
  • No film frames in either, by design
  • Everything organised by angle
Theodore needs

Your pass through the angle markers first, so it is built on a clean list.

Effort
Watch out

The tips are generic how-tos, not claims about how the film was made.

Say it"Build F from the angle log."
Theodore · Field Guide · Options E and F07
G
SHOT-LIST BUILDER
BUILT
What it does

Give Theodore a shot list or a script and it creates the bins (A-Cam, B-Cam, Audio, Graphics), one timeline per lesson, a marker per shot at its planned length, a slate for every shot, and the ID list production slates with.

You get
  • IDs like M01-L01-S01 on every shot
  • Pick a skin: Neon, Hollywood or Full Frame
  • Reference stills drop straight into each slate
Theodore needs

The shot list: lesson, shot type, description, planned length.

Effort
Watch out

The 21-shot sample is invented. Director, DP and editor read "TBD" until you supply real names.

Say it"Build G from this shot list."
H
SWAP-IN LOOP
DRY-RUN ONLY
What it does

Reads the slate on each take with Resolve's Slate ID, matches it to its placeholder, drops it in at the planned length with the lavs linked, and keeps unused takes in the bin.

You get
  • A first assembly that builds itself as you shoot
  • Planned length vs actual on every shot
  • Unclear slates flagged for you, never guessed
Theodore needs

A few real slated takes and the lav files to test on safely.

Effort
Watch out

Only the read-only matcher exists. Whether Resolve's slate reader fills Scene and Take from a script is still untested.

Say it"Set up H, here are my slated takes."

House rules

What Theodore never does

  • No surprise renders. Nothing is rendered unless you ask.
  • No deleting. Old builds are renamed as checkpoints and filed in Timelines/Working.
  • Read-back after every build. Clip counts, gaps and markers are checked, then shown.
  • Stops when Resolve stops. If Resolve refuses writes, Theodore tells you instead of forcing it.
  • Lavs travel with the video. Every cut carries its mic audio, linked.
  • Honest labels. If a shot cannot be seen clearly, it says UNVERIFIED rather than guessing.
  • Film frames stay private. They never go into a student guide or sheet.
Theodore · Field Guide10
Straight talk

Known limits

  • Cuts in the dark. Door swings, whip pans and light flicker fool the detector. Three were fixed after you caught them; more may remain.
  • Angle height. Defaults to eye level when the camera height cannot be seen.
  • Sound. The screen recording has no audio, so sound is unverified.
  • The John Wick study uses a fan upload recorded on screen. It is private study material, not for students.
  • GitHub is not connected, so saved work lives on this Mac only.
  • Chrome setting. "Allow JavaScript from Apple Events" is on for the video work. You can switch it off in View > Developer.
