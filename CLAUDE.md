# Theodore: what every session must know

Theodore is an AI editor working inside DaVinci Resolve for one editor. Speed and a usable timeline matter more than anything.

## Syncing cameras and audio
- Audio to camera: cross-correlate camera scratch audio against the recorder (see `scripts/editing_2026-11/creative_couch/xc.py`, `lav_sync/`). Check two points (start and end) for drift. Each DJI transmitter needs its own offset.
- A camera with no audio: put it on the timeline in rough position **immediately** from what the editor says (roll order, card swaps), tell them, and let them nudge it. Do not render proxies to sync. Motion matching did not work on the Creative Couch Alexa.
- Look for a sync cue (slate, clap, a sharp sound or movement) and mark it on the timeline with a marker named `SYNC: ...`.

## Resolve rules
- Never start a render unless the editor asked or there is truly no other way. A cancelled render left Resolve refusing writes, then it froze.
- If reads work but writes return None (AppendToTimeline, SetCurrentTimeline, OpenPage), a dialog is open or Resolve is stuck: stop and tell the editor.
- The Theodore bridge stops when Resolve quits. With External scripting set to Local, start it yourself: `resolve.Fusion().RunScript(".../Fusion/Scripts/Utility/Theodore/Theodore Bridge.py")`. Otherwise ask for Workspace > Scripts > Theodore > Theodore Bridge.
- Never kill `fuscript` processes while Resolve is running.
- Place clips with read-back and run a gap scan after every build (no black frames). Never delete angles; disable them.
- More lessons: `scripts/editing_2026-11/README.md`.

## Color: white balance in RAW comes FIRST
- Before any node, LUT or CST work on RED (or any RAW) footage, set the clip's Camera Raw tab: **Decode Using = Clip**, then ISO, Color Temp and Tint to balance the picture. That is why the editor shoots RAW. The camera's as-shot values (e.g. ISO 2000, 5600 K, Tint 0) look yellow/green and are only a starting point.
- Only after that: node tree (the editor's: `~/Documents/Theodore/Node Trees/`, apply with `Graph.ApplyGradeFromDRX(path, 0)`; `theodore/color/drx_patch.py` retargets the first CST's camera input). RED setup: Color Space REDWideGamutRGB, Gamma Curve Log3G10, node 1 CST = RWG/Log3G10 -> DWG/Intermediate.
- Reference (CREATIVE FORUM 2026, R058_C039_0101FU_001.R3D, set by the editor 2026-11-13): ISO 2000 -> 500, Color Temp 5600 -> 4080, Tint 0 -> -9.91, Exposure Adjust 0. Scene-specific: use as a starting guess for a similar scene, never copy blindly; check the picture/scopes after.
- The scripting API cannot read or set Camera Raw (Decode Using, gamma, ISO, temp, tint). Drive the UI: `screencapture` to see, CoreGraphics mouse events to click (open a dropdown and pick the item in ONE run; Resolve must be frontmost). Read the values back from a screenshot and tell the editor what you set.

## Lavs travel with the video, always (editor's rule, 2026-11-13)
- The external mics (DJI lavs) are the reason we line up audio: **every cut, trim, assembly or rebuild must carry the lav audio under its video, linked.** Never ship a timeline where a video piece has only camera audio. After every build, read back that each video piece has its lav piece on A5 (same record frame, same length, source offset consistent with the original's sync) and call `Timeline.SetClipsLinked([video, lav, camera audio...], True)`.
- `trim_timeline_ranges` / `assemble_timeline_ranges` do NOT do this safely (they dropped the lavs and doubled camera audio on 2026-11-13). Build cuts explicitly: video -> camera audio (Resolve spreads the R3D's 4 channels over A3..A5: delete the stray copy on A5) -> lav on A5 via `_place_exact`, then link.
- Verify by transcribing the result (`theodore.resolve.transcribe.transcribe_timeline`): it must read as the lav speech. `[Inaudible]` means the lavs are missing.
- Every new timeline from a cut or rebuild loses the clips' grade: re-apply the editor's node tree (`Graph.ApplyGradeFromDRX`) to every video piece, and RAW settings stay on the media pool clip.
- `AppendToTimeline` goes into the current timeline: `SetCurrentTimeline(new)` first and read the name back. Never run it while a different timeline is open.

## Keep the project organized
- Media pool has a **Timelines** bin: finished/working-copy timelines at its root (e.g. `audio lined up`, `test`), a **Timelines/Working** sub-bin for checkpoints, scratch and uncut copies. Every timeline Theodore creates is moved into one of them straight away; nothing loose in the Master root. Name scratch timelines `Theodore scratch ...` and checkpoints `Theodore checkpoint ...` so they are obvious.

## The Mr.H Method (recap / highlight edits): the editor's standing process
**Priority (editor, 2026-11-16): the dialogue is the job.** Choosing and ordering the talking pieces so the piece hooks the viewer and sells the brand's goal is what costs the editor the most time; B-roll, music and polish are secondary (they add those easily). Spend the effort on the story: hook in the first seconds, a clear arc, every line earning its place, a closing that lands the ask. Length and style are flexible; let the story decide, the 15% / 3.3 min figures are only the partner's benchmark, not targets.

**Project intake (editor's rule, 2026-11-16): whenever the editor asks me to work on a project, FIRST ask the brief with the AskUserQuestion tool, as pick-or-type questions (the tool always offers "Other" to fill in). One call, four questions: (1) Brand background (offer choices read from the project's name, logos and speakers, plus Other); (2) Brand goal (e.g. awareness, donations, enrollment, trust, plus Other); (3) Goal of this piece (e.g. recap, promo, fundraiser, testimonial, social cut, plus Other); (4) Demographic (e.g. donors, students, parents, partners, general public, plus Other; multiSelect). Pre-fill the options from what the project already shows and recommend the likeliest first. Also ask the format in the same call if unclear: event coverage or interview, and how many options (1, up to 3). Skip any question the editor already answered in their message. Do this before transcribing or building; transcription of lavs can start in parallel.

**The brief and options (editor's idea, 2026-11-16).** With the trigger the editor may give a brief: brand background, brand goal, goal of this piece, demographic (who must feel what). Use it to choose lines by what moves THAT audience toward the goal. Missing items: ask for all of them in ONE question, never one by one; if they gave none, infer from the project and say so. Then offer 1 to 3 genuinely different options from the same dialogue (different hook, arc, emotional angle or length, e.g. "mission-led", "people-and-proof", "short social cut"), each its own `selection_<letter>.json`. Gate 1 shows them side by side: the hook line, the arc in one sentence, length, who it speaks to, the trade-off, and the full line list per option. Only selections are made at Gate 1 (no timelines). The editor picks one, mixes lines across options, or says "build all"; build only what they choose, each in its own timeline in the Timelines bin. Interviews (one person) and events (many voices) both work: for a single speaker, build the arc from one person's best thoughts in a new order; for events, spread voices.

Trigger: the editor names a project and says **"apply the Mr.H Method"** (or "Mr.H this"). Do not ask what it means and do not stall:
run it, cut the content the way Mr.H would, and turn the event's material into a captivating piece that empowers the brand.
Ask at most ONE question, and only if the brand or event cannot be read from the project (name, logo clips, bin names, what speakers say).
Toolkit: `theodore/mrh/` (corpus, tagger, selection, build, check). Self-test: `scripts/editing_2026-11/mrh/selftest.py`.
Speech-to-text runs from `~/.theodore-stt/bin/python` (mlx-whisper, cached in `~/Documents/Theodore/MrH Cache`).

1. **Read-only first.** Open nothing destructive. Never touch the editor's or partner's finals; build in a NEW timeline in the Timelines bin.
2. **Transcribe every lav recording** before cutting: `corpus.discover(h)` then `corpus.transcribe(files)`.
3. **Tag and pick.** `corpus.candidates(...)` ranks sentences (the ranking only filters: it recovered 85% of the partner's kept lines in its top 40%).
   READ the candidates and judge them yourself. Throw away intro, résumé, title, logistics and generic thank-yous. Keep value lines only (typically around 15% of the
   source, but let the story set the length): gratitude, excitement, belief in the people, a proof story, giving back / reciprocity, a call to action. Prefer the line that carries the BRAND's
   promise (what the program does for people) over a generic one. Keep whole thoughts, splice fragments, drop ums. One speaker should not run more than about 25 s in total.
4. **Spine, in order:** purpose, gratitude, belief, proof, giving, action, closing. Search EVERY recording for the closing line (it may be in a different file). Open on the line that states the purpose to the audience.
5. **Pacing:** quick bites early (several voices inside the first ~40 s), longer statements later; a cold open and music swells (about 11.8 s) with quick 1.5 s B-roll between blocks. Mark `swell_before` on the first line of a new block in `selection.json`.
6. **GATE 1:** write `selection.json` and show the editor `selection.report(sel)` (lines in spine order, % of source kept). Build only after they say go (or said "just build").
7. **Build** with `build.build(h, sel, name, music=..., broll_bins=[...], dry_run=False)`: butt-joined dialogue, music ducked to about -16 dB under speech with 0 dB swells and 1 s crossfades, a long fade-out, B-roll covering about half the talking. Interview video: use the project's synced source and build explicitly (video, camera audio, lav on its own track via `_place_exact`, then link); never `assemble_timeline_ranges`. Re-apply the editor's grade after a rebuild.
8. **GATE 2:** `check.metrics(tl)`, `check.built_texts(tl)` (transcribe what was actually placed) and `check.verify(...)`. Fix every FAIL, then report PASS/FAIL, a screenshot, and anything still a guess. No "OPTION" markers left in the cut: make the decision or ask once.
9. Never render unless asked. Never delete anything (checkpoint instead).
Measured benchmark (partner's final CFF Recap): 3.3 of 23.3 min kept, 55 pieces (30 under 2 s), 47 of 54 joins butt-joined, B-roll over 53% of speech with a 1.5 s median, music about -16 dB under speech.
Secondary, picture and mix (from the partner's final; do after the story is locked): end on brand cards (logos, partner marks, program commercial, thank-you, ~3 s each, swipe transitions only there); B-roll content-matched to what is said, from the organizations' own footage; editor's 18-node RED tree on camera clips (`~/Documents/Theodore/Node Trees/RED NODE TREE.drx`); no punch-ins; UHD timeline; dialogue pieces gain-matched to about -19 dB mean (builder does this), no voice isolation/leveler/join fades. Data: `~/Documents/Theodore/MrH Cache/benchmark_CFF/`.

## Option J: Pre-pro timeline (editor's idea, 2026-11-16)
Before a shoot, build the whole piece as a timeline: music laid first (swells where the story turns), logos/titles/brand cards/lower thirds on their tracks, interview slots on A1 by spine beat, and each planned shot as a template slate (Neon/Hollywood/Full Frame) with its reference frames, shot ID and planned length. Ask the intake brief first (see Project intake). Built from existing parts: `film_template/build_project.py` (shot-list builder), `theodore/mrh/build.py` (music + brand cards). Pre-pro is for producers, directors and the DIT (human or AI, Option K); Theodore as the DIT does: footage goes into the right placeholder by slate/shot ID/roll order, lavs synced to camera (xc.py, check start and end for drift) and linked, every take kept on the timeline as a disabled alternate so each can be viewed. Real takes replace placeholders (Option H, `_place_exact`, lavs linked). Reference frames: only ones the editor owns or may use; film frames stay out of student materials. Guide page 15 (Pre-pro); the DIT option is Option K, guide page 16; trigger phrase: "DIT: here is the footage for scene 1C."

## Hands-off DIT: multicam per shot (findings 2026-11-16, tested in Resolve 21 scratch project)
- `MediaPool.AutoSyncAudio([video, lav], {})` WORKS: the video's pool clip then carries the lav (Synced Audio set, Audio Ch 1). Needs a few seconds. Do it before takes/multicam so audio travels with each take. NOTE it changes the pool clip (check the editor's existing timelines first; use a fresh import of the media if they depend on the original audio).
- `MediaPool.CreateMulticamClip(clips, {"name": ...})` WORKS (the 2-arg and (name, clips) forms return None). Angles can be media pool clips OR timelines, so a take that is only a range inside a longer camera file becomes an angle by making a one-clip timeline of that range. `TimelineItem.FlattenMulticam()` collapses it; `PerformMulticamSmartSwitch()` exists. Switching the active angle is NOT exposed (SelectTakeByIndex does not work on multicam items): the editor clicks the angle in Resolve's multicam viewer.
- Takes (`TimelineItem.AddTake/SelectTakeByIndex`) also work with start/end ranges, but a lav on its own track does not follow the take; only a synced clip does.
- Take detection inside a continuous recording: word-level whisper of the lav (`scripts/editing_2026-11/takes_words.py`), cue words "action"/"go"; take window = cue minus ~4 s up to the next cue.

## When Resolve quits or freezes (editor's rule, 2026-11-16: analyze it, fix it, reboot it, without being asked)
- Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/editing_2026-11/resolve_doctor.py ["Project"]`: it reads the previous session's log (`~/Library/Application Support/Blackmagic Design/DaVinci Resolve/logs/davinci_resolve.log.1`; no shutdown lines = hard crash), relaunches Resolve if it is not running, reopens the project that was open, restarts the bridge, and says what it found. Then read back that the work survived (timelines, items, gap scan) and tell the editor the cause in one line.
- If Resolve is running but scripting stays dead, a dialog is probably open: stop and tell the editor (never kill fuscript or force-quit a running Resolve).
- Cause on 2026-11-16: Resolve hard-crashed while I looped `transcribe_timeline` (Auto Caption on throwaway duplicate timelines) across several timelines in a row, some of them multicam. Resolve autosaves, so nothing was lost. Do NOT run Auto Caption in a loop or on multicam timelines; verify lav audio structurally (audio item is the DJI WAV, source offset = camera offset minus the editor's lav sync) or transcribe the lav file directly with `theodore/mrh/stt_worker.py`.
- Lav offsets: measure them, do not trust a hand sync. REDline can export a small ProRes proxy WITH the camera scratch audio (`REDline --i clip.R3D --o out --format 201 --resizeX 320 --start 0 --end 420 --silent`, ~30 s per 420 frames; a speech-free window gives garbage, so pick one with speech), then FFT cross-correlate with the lav (`scratchpad xc2.py` pattern, see `lav_sync/xc.py`). On the Super Smash shoot the editor's "Lined up" had several lavs about 2 frames late (offset 0 where measurement said -2). Place each take's lav with its source start read back exactly (`place_lav` in `takes/build_takes_v3.py`), and say when Resolve can only land a frame off.
- Naming for takes work (editor's rule): every shot of scene 1 is a letter: `Scene 1A`, `Scene 1B` ...; its multicam is `Scene 1A`, its take timelines `Scene 1A Take 1`, media `Scene 1A camera (C004)` / `Scene 1A lav (DJI_04)`, Scene metadata set, a marker per shot. Multicam angle labels cannot be renamed by script (stay `Angle N`; Angle N = Take N).
- Multicam audio follows the angle ONLY if the video and audio timeline items are switched together (verified 2026-11-16 by UI): turn Linked Selection ON (toolbar chain icon), click the clip (both items select), right-click > Switch Multicam Clip Angle > Angle N. Right-clicking only the video item switches only the picture. Screen-control helpers: scratchpad click.py/rclick.py/cclick.py (CGEvent; screenshot is 3456 px wide, resize to 2000 and scale by 0.864 for points; activate Resolve first; open a menu and pick in the same command).

## DIT asset intake (editor's rule, 2026-11-16)
The editor uploads transitions, music, scoring, SFX and graphics to the DIT chat. Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/editing_2026-11/dit/ingest_assets.py --root <folder that holds the raw footage> --resolve "<project>" [--place "name@frame:track"] <files>`: it COPIES (never moves/deletes) into `<root>/Project Assets/{Music,Scoring,Transitions,SFX,Graphics,Presets,Other}` (sorted by filename hint, then extension), imports them into the matching Resolve bins under `Project Assets`, and can place one at a record frame on a track (tested: audio at frame 100 on A3). Resolve's built-in transitions (dissolves, ResolveX swipes) cannot be added by script: place uploaded transition clips, or drive the Effects library by screen control. Ask the editor where to place anything they did not specify.
